# Chapter 1 - Why Houses Become Obsolete

> A house should not become obsolete simply because the world around it changes.

For centuries, homes have been designed with a single objective: to provide shelter. While construction methods have improved dramatically, the underlying philosophy has changed surprisingly little. Most homes are still conceived as static objects: built once, occupied for decades, and eventually renovated, extended, or demolished when they no longer meet the needs of their occupants.

Project Horizon begins with a different assumption.

Rather than treating a house as a finished product, it treats it as a long-life platform.

The structure provides permanence.  
The enclosure provides environmental protection.  
The services provide comfort.  
The interiors provide flexibility.  
Technology provides intelligence.

Each layer has a different expected lifespan, and each should be designed accordingly.

## The Problem With Today's Houses

Most modern homes are optimised for the day they are completed. Little consideration is given to what happens ten, twenty, or fifty years later.

When a family grows, rooms are often too rigidly arranged to adapt without structural alteration. When technology advances, new wiring and services must be threaded through walls and floors never intended to accommodate them. When heating systems are replaced, the building itself often dictates what can and cannot be installed.

The result is predictable: buildings that remain structurally sound become functionally obsolete.

## Houses as Systems

A successful system separates long-life components from short-life components.

Aircraft are designed this way.  
Commercial buildings are designed this way.  
Modern software is designed this way.  
Yet houses often are not.

A residential building contains elements with very different expected lifespans:

- Foundations may last more than one hundred years.
- Structural steel framing may last for generations.
- External cladding may require replacement after fifty years.
- Windows may need renewal after forty years.
- Mechanical systems may be replaced every twenty years.
- Smart-home technology may become obsolete within ten years.

Project Horizon adopts a layered approach in which each component is designed to be inspected, maintained, and replaced with minimal disruption to the others.

## The Project Horizon Principle

**A house should become more valuable over time because it can adapt, not less valuable because it cannot.**
